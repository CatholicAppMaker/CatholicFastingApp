# Catholic Fasting — 30-Day Growth Sprint

**Objective:** grow new users and new paid subscribers by 2–3× in the next 30 days, without
compromising the app's local-first, no-ad-tracking promise.

## Day 0: establish the scorecard

Use App Store Connect as the source of truth. Record the last 30 days, then set both the 2×
commitment target and the 3× stretch target.

| Metric | Baseline (last 30 days) | 2× target | 3× target | Daily pace for 2× |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First-time downloads |  | baseline × 2 | baseline × 3 | target ÷ 30 |
| Product-page conversion |  | improve, not just increase spend | improve | — |
| Onboarding completions |  | baseline × 2 | baseline × 3 | target ÷ 30 |
| New paid subscriptions |  | baseline × 2 | baseline × 3 | target ÷ 30 |
| Trial-to-paid / purchase conversion |  | hold or improve | improve | — |
| Spend |  |  |  | — |
| Cost per download |  | within contribution margin | within contribution margin | — |

Review this table every Monday. Do not call increased impressions or page views "growth" unless
they produce first-time downloads and activated users.

## Growth model

The 30-day goal depends on four linked levers:

1. **Qualified visits:** people searching for Catholic fasting guidance or reached through trusted
   Catholic communities.
2. **Product-page conversion:** screenshots and the first three lines must answer “What should I do
   today?” before explaining features.
3. **Activation:** a new user selects language and region, finishes setup, and takes one meaningful
   action (views Today, logs a discipline day, or starts a fast).
4. **Paid conversion:** after core free value is clear, users see Guided Seasonal Formation as a
   coherent weekly rhythm—not as a restriction on basic Catholic guidance.

## 30-day execution calendar

### Days 1–3 — release and measurement

- Publish or verify the production App Store listing; test its link from an iPhone not signed into a
  developer account.
- Put the existing localized screenshots and current metadata into the live listing. Lead with
  “Know today’s Catholic fast, abstinence, feast, or Friday penance,” then show tracking and
  private/local-only trust.
- Create one campaign row per source in the scorecard: Apple Ads search, X organic, parish/newsletter,
  creator, and direct/unknown.
- Capture baseline screenshots of the product page, subscription offers, and onboarding. This makes
  later conversion changes auditable.

### Days 4–10 — test two acquisition lanes

**Paid: Apple Ads search**

- Start with exact/high-intent terms: `catholic fasting`, `catholic abstinence`, `friday penance`,
  `catholic lent`, and `catholic fasting calendar`.
- Separate brand, high-intent Catholic terms, and exploratory terms into different ad groups. Use a
  small, fixed test budget and pause a term when it spends without downloads.
- Make the ad promise match the listing: daily guidance, planning, tracking, and private local data.
  Do not make medical, weight-loss, or authority-over-local-bishop claims.

**Organic: trusted distribution**

- Publish three short posts per week around the practical Friday question: “What does Friday
  penance mean this week?” “How do I plan the next required fast day?” and “A calm, local-first
  Catholic fasting rhythm.” Each post gets the production App Store link and one screenshot.
- Send a concise, personal note to 25 aligned parish newsletters, Catholic campus ministries,
  diocesan communications contacts, and small creators. Offer a ready-to-paste two-sentence blurb
  and screenshots; never imply an endorsement.
- Ask five early users for an honest store review after they have completed a real milestone. The
  app now makes a one-time system review request after a user reaches their third successful fast;
  Apple decides whether to show the sheet.

### Days 11–21 — keep winners, improve activation

- Every three days, rank channels by first-time downloads, product-page conversion, and new paid
  subscribers—not clicks alone.
- Move the next three days of budget to the best search terms. Keep at least 20% of the paid test
  budget reserved for a challenger ad group until day 18.
- If downloads rise but onboarding completion does not, replace the first screenshot or opening
  description with the exact promise used in the winning ad. If activation rises but paid conversion
  is weak, test premium framing and screenshots before discounting.
- Repost the strongest organic asset in English, Spanish, and French Canadian only after a native
  speaker checks the copy. The app already supports these three core flows; use that as an inclusion
  benefit, not a claim of universal local-rule coverage.

### Days 22–30 — scale deliberately

- Pause channels that miss the agreed cost-per-download ceiling for two consecutive reviews.
- Increase the best channel's daily budget gradually while product-page conversion and paid
  conversion hold steady. Do not double budget in a single day.
- Publish a “30-day rhythm” follow-up for users who installed but have not purchased: required
  guidance remains free; Premium keeps seasonal planning, reminders, reflection, and review in one
  workflow.
- At day 30, compare every scorecard row with baseline, preserve winning campaign structure, and
  write down the next seasonal angle before Lent/Advent demand arrives.

## Decision rules

- A channel is a winner only when it produces installs within the cost ceiling *and* its paid-user
  conversion is not materially worse than the overall average.
- Do not add third-party behavioral analytics merely to attribute this sprint; that would contradict
  the current App Privacy posture. Use App Store Connect campaign and source reports plus the
  existing local funnel export for product decisions.
- Do not gate required Catholic guidance, store-review access, or safety/dispensation guidance behind
  Premium.
- Treat the 3× mark as a stretch outcome. If the 2× daily pace is missed for seven consecutive days,
  correct distribution or listing conversion immediately rather than simply spending more.
